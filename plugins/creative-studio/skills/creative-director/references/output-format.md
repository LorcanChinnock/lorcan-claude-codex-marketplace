# Output Format: Creative Director

The output is a single, flowing design brief document. Not a slide deck outline. Not a status report. A piece of thinking that a designer can walk into a project holding.

---

## Section Order

Follow this order exactly. Use horizontal rules (`---`) between sections. Use the exact heading names shown.

---

### THE BRIEF, REFRAMED

One or two sentences that restate the problem as a creative opportunity or a creative tension. This is not a summary of what the user said. It is the problem, shifted to reveal what's interesting about it.

**Annotation:**
- Good: surfaces the tension or the opportunity lurking in the requirements
- Good: is specific to this brief, not a generic restating of "design X for Y"
- Fails: "Design a mobile app for fitness tracking" — that's what the user already said
- Fails: any mention of what the solution will look like at this stage

---

### WHAT TO BURN

A list of specifically named design tropes from the live research this session. 3–5 items. Each item should name the pattern specifically enough that a designer would recognise it on sight.

**Format:**

```
- [Name of the trope] — [one sentence on what it looks like and why it's overused or unsuitable here]
```

**Annotation:**
- Names must come from the live research fetched this session. Not from training data.
- Specific enough to be actionable: "the bento grid layout with pastel cards" beats "bento grids"
- Say why it's wrong *for this brief*, not just why it's generic

---

### CONCEPTS

Three named design directions. Present each as its own subsection.

**Format for each concept:**

```
#### [CONCEPT NAME]

[3–5 sentences of concept description]
```

**Annotation for concept description:**
- Lead with the idea or the quality, not the visual treatment
- Name what cultural moment, material, or reference it draws from — something found in the live research
- Say what it rejects from the "What to Burn" list
- Describe the experience of using or encountering it — spatial logic, emotional register, pace
- Do not describe hex codes, font names, or design system decisions at this stage
- No bullet points inside concept descriptions

**What makes concepts strategically distinct:**
- Different emotional registers (austere vs. expansive, intimate vs. institutional)
- Different reference worlds (industrial vs. archival, editorial vs. environmental)
- Different temporal logics (immediate vs. slow, dense vs. sparse)
- Not: same idea with different colour moods

---

### REFERENCES

3–5 specific pieces of work, projects, publications, or visual sources found in the live research this session.

**Format:**

```
- [Name or title] — [what specifically is interesting about it for this brief, in one sentence]
```

**Annotation:**
- Must link to or describe something found in live research, not recalled from training data
- Say what is specifically relevant — not just "this is a well-regarded studio"
- Avoid generic "inspiration" framing. Say what quality to borrow and from where in the piece

---

### THE PICK

State which concept is recommended. Give one specific reason grounded in the brief, the research, or a named constraint. One paragraph, no longer.

If the brief was thin, append one sentence naming the assumption: "This works if [X]. If [Y], revisit [Concept N]."

**Annotation:**
- No hedging: "all three have merit" is not a pick
- No false balance: do not qualify the pick with equal praise for the other two
- The reason should be specific: not "it suits the brand" but "because the research shows [X trope] is saturating this market, and this concept is the only one that avoids it entirely at the strategy level, not just the surface level"

---

### NEXT

2–3 concrete next moves. Prototype, test, define, commission, decide — specific actions, not open-ended suggestions.

**Format:**

```
1. [Action verb] + [specific thing to do or produce]
2. ...
3. ...
```

**Annotation:**
- Specific enough that a designer knows what to produce or open next
- Avoid vague directives: "explore typography" fails — "typeset three lines of the UI copy in [quality described in the concept] to test register" works
- The last item can name the open question to resolve before the next round

---

## Prose Rules Inside Sections

Within any section, every sentence should:

- Say something specific to this brief, not something true of all design projects
- Have a subject that is not "we" or "you" or "the design"
- Avoid adverbs as hedges: "perhaps", "potentially", "could potentially" — commit

The "What to Burn" and "References" sections use the list format above. All other sections should read as continuous prose paragraphs, not bullet lists.
